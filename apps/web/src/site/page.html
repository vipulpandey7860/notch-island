Your notch,
put to work.

Notch Island turns the black strip at the top of your MacBook into a small workspace: today's tasks, a note for the day, what's playing, what you've copied and what your coding agents are up to. It's native, free and open source, and a tiny robot lives on it.

Get Notch Island See what it does

For MacBooks running macOS 14 or later. Free and open source under the GPL-3.0.

Type it the way you'd say it

Call mom tomorrow at 5pm goes onto tomorrow's list with a 5 PM reminder. Before you press Return, the line above the box shows exactly what will be saved.

Try it in the island up there: click the box and type a task with a day or a time in it.

  • Tasks without a time stay on today's list until you finish them. A reminder you missed turns red and says Overdue.
  • ⌥N opens the island from any app with the cursor in the box. The app you're in stays in front, and esc hands the keyboard back.
  • Each day has a note that saves as you type. ⌘↩ turns the line you're on into a task.
  • Double-click a task to edit it. Its menu sets a reminder, moves it to another day or deletes it.

When it's time, the notch says so

At the reminder time the island drops down with a chime, whatever app you're in, and the robot waves at you. Snooze it for five minutes or tick it off. Nothing goes to Notification Center.

Look up at the notch, and try its buttons

Keep an eye on Claude Code

Connect Claude Code in Settings, and while it works, the project and how long it's been at it sit beside the notch, with the robot typing at its desk. When it wants to run something, the notch shows exactly what, with Allow, Deny and In terminal. Codex tells you when it finishes a turn.

Allow only appears when the notch can show the whole request. A command over several lines, a file outside your project, or anything with hidden characters goes to the terminal, where you can read all of it.

Park files in the notch

Drag files up to the notch and let go. They wait there while you find the folder or app they're going to, then you drag them back out. On the same disk they move; hold ⌥ to copy.

  • Drop on AirDrop instead and they're on their way.
  • Right-click a file to share, compress, copy its path or show it in Finder. Drag all takes the whole stack.
  • Safari and Chrome downloads show a progress ring beside the notch, and land on the shelf when they finish.

Drag one up into the notch. With a keyboard, press Return on a file.

What you copied, a click away

Everything you copy and every screenshot you take collects in the Clips tab, next to the notepad. Click one to copy it again, or drag it straight into another app.

  • Take a screenshot and the notch tells you it's there.
  • Clips live in memory only, so they're gone when you quit. Anything a password manager marks as secret is never kept.

Whatever's playing, from any app

Spotify, Music or a tab in your browser: the artwork sits beside the notch and the robot dances. Open the island for a player with progress, play, skip and seek. Swipe two fingers across it to skip, or scroll over the bar to scrub.

Five minutes' warning

Your next meeting comes from any calendar on your Mac, Google included. Five minutes before it starts, the island shows how long you've got, and the open island gets a Join button for the Meet, Zoom or Teams call. Meetings you declined are skipped.

Look up at the notch

Timers you just type

Type tea 4 min in the task box and press Return. The countdown sits beside the notch, and when it's up the notch drops down with +1 min and Done. Call mom in 20 min is still a task with a reminder.

For longer stretches, right-click the island for a focus timer: 25 minutes and a 5-minute break, or whatever lengths you set. The robot sits down at its desk and types until it rings.

Look up at the notch

And the small things

  • It says Charging when you plug in, and the open island shows your battery and network speed.
  • Every display gets its own island. External displays, and your MacBook with the lid closed, get a notch-sized one at the top.
  • Settings can open it at login, and switch off the robot, meetings or network speed.

The robot that lives there

It lives around the notch on magnet boots, so it can walk anywhere along the edge. It's beside the notch right now. Hover over a pose below to see it move.

It stays on your Mac

There's no account, no cloud and no analytics. Your tasks and notes are plain files in your Library folder, readable in any text editor. Clips stay in memory. Claude Code and Codex reach it through a socket in your Library folder that only you can open, and if Notch Island isn't running they carry on as before.

It's written in Swift with AppKit and uses no third-party code. One honest note: macOS only tells Apple's own apps what's playing, so Notch Island asks through the copy of perl that ships with macOS. If a future macOS closes that gap, now playing goes and everything else keeps working.

Get it

Download the latest release, unzip it and move Notch Island to your Applications folder. It's free, and if it earns a place in your menu bar, you can sponsor it.

Download Notch IslandSponsor it on GitHub
  1. The first time you open it, macOS says it can't check the app, because it isn't notarized by Apple (that needs a paid developer account). Open System Settings → Privacy & Security, scroll down, and click Open Anyway. You only do this once.
  2. After that it keeps itself up to date: once a day it checks for a new version, shows you what's new, and installs it when you click Install and Relaunch. Every update is checked against this project's release key first.

Or build it yourself. It takes about a minute and needs only Xcode's Command Line Tools, not all of Xcode.

# once, if you don't have the Command Line Tools
$ xcode-select --install

$ git clone https://github.com/vipulpandey7860/notch-island.git
$ cd notch-island/apps/mac && ./build.sh run
